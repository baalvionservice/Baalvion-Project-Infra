# Stock Market Circuit Breakers

> *"When markets tumble, circuit breakers pause the frenzy and give investors a breather."*

## What Are Circuit Breakers?

Circuit breakers are **temporary trading halts** triggered when a major index (e.g., the S&P 500, Dow Jones Industrial Average, or Nasdaq Composite) experiences a rapid price decline. Their purpose is to:
- Prevent panic‑selling spirals.
- Provide market participants time to digest news.
- Preserve orderly market functioning.

## How They Work – The Tiered System

The U.S. equity markets employ a **three‑level** circuit‑breaker framework. Each level corresponds to a specific percentage drop from the previous day’s closing price and has a predefined halt duration.

| Level | Decline Threshold (Intraday) | Halt Length |
|-------|-------------------------------|------------|
| **Level 1** | 7 % | 15 minutes (if before 3:25 PM ET) |
| **Level 2** | 13 % | 15 minutes (if before 3:25 PM ET) |
| **Level 3** | 20 % | Market closed for the remainder of the day |

> **Note:** After 3:25 PM ET, only a Level 3 halt (full‑day closure) can occur; lower‑level halts are skipped to avoid unnecessary interruptions near the close.

## Why Do They Matter?
- **Investor Protection:** Halts can prevent a cascade of margin calls and forced liquidations.
- **Price Discovery:** By pausing, the market gains time to incorporate new information, reducing mispricing.
- **Liquidity Management:** Allows market makers to re‑balance inventories and quote more accurate prices.

## Potential Downsides
- **Trading Disruption:** Active traders may miss entry/exit windows.
- **Volatility Spike Post‑Halt:** Pent‑up orders can cause a sudden price swing when trading resumes.
- **Strategic Exploitation:** Some high‑frequency firms may anticipate halts to manipulate order flow.

## How to Trade During a Halt
1. **Stay Informed:** Watch real‑time news feeds; the trigger is usually news‑driven.
2. **Review Order Types:** Market orders may be canceled; consider limit orders to control execution price.
3. **Assess the Reason:** If the halt follows a macroeconomic announcement, expect continued volatility after the restart.
4. **Risk‑Manage:** Tighten stop‑losses (once allowed) and adjust position sizing.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
**Q:** *What happens to open orders when a halt occurs?*  
**A:** Most exchanges cancel pending market orders. Limit orders remain but are queued.

**Q:** *Do all exchanges use the same thresholds?*  
**A:** No. The U.S. equity markets follow the SEC‑established percentages, while European and Asian markets have their own rules.

**Q:** *Can circuit breakers be triggered by a single stock’s move?*  
**A:** No – they are index‑wide measures. Individual stock halts are governed by separate “limit‑up/limit‑down” mechanisms.

## Bottom Line
Circuit breakers act as the market’s **emergency brake**, giving participants a moment to breathe, reassess, and avoid a disorderly crash. Understanding their tiers, historical contexts, and trading implications can help both novice and seasoned investors navigate volatile periods with confidence.
