# Cruise Ship Accident Lawyer in Florida

Florida is home to several of the world's busiest cruise ports, including PortMiami, Port Everglades in Fort Lauderdale, and Port Canaveral near Orlando, as well as the corporate headquarters of a number of major cruise lines. Because of this concentration, Florida — and its federal courts in particular — play a central role in cruise ship accident litigation nationwide, whether or not the injured passenger lives in the state. This guide explains why. It is general legal information, not legal advice about any specific injury.

## Florida's Role in Cruise Ship Injury Claims

Most major cruise lines' standard passenger ticket contracts include a forum-selection clause designating a specific court — very often a federal court in Florida — as the exclusive venue for any lawsuit arising from a passenger injury, regardless of where the passenger lives or boarded the ship. This practice was upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court in *Carnival Cruise Lines, Inc. v. Shute*, 499 U.S. 585 (1991). As a result, a large proportion of U.S. cruise ship injury litigation is filed in Florida's federal courts, which have developed substantial experience with maritime passenger claims. ## Florida's Cruise Ports

Florida's major cruise ports each serve different lines and itineraries, and a passenger's departure port can be relevant to which shore excursions, tender operations, and embarkation procedures were involved in an accident, even though it is typically the ticket contract — not the departure port — that determines where a lawsuit must be filed. Our [cruise ship accident lawyer guide](/cruise-ship-accident-lawyer/) explains the general legal framework that applies regardless of departure port.

## The Cruise Ticket Contract and Florida Venue Clauses

Federal law, 46 U.S.C. § 30508, sets minimum protections for passengers regardless of what a ticket contract says — a cruise line cannot shorten the notice period below six months or the suit-filing deadline below one year. Beyond those minimums, ticket contracts commonly include additional terms, including the Florida forum-selection clause discussed above, that a cruise ship accident lawyer will review closely as part of any claim.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do all cruise lines require lawsuits to be filed in Florida?** Not necessarily every line, but a large share of major cruise lines — many headquartered in South Florida — do designate a Florida court in their ticket contracts. The specific clause should always be confirmed in your own ticket contract.

**Does it matter which Florida port I departed from?** The departure port itself generally isn't what determines where a lawsuit must be filed — that's set by the ticket contract's forum-selection clause — though it can be relevant to the facts of an embarkation or shore excursion accident.

**What if I'm not a Florida resident?** That's very common in cruise litigation — passengers from all over the country and world often end up litigating claims in Florida's federal courts because of their ticket contract's forum clause, not because they live there.
