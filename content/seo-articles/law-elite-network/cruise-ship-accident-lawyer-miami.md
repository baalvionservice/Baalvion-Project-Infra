# Cruise Ship Accident Lawyer in Miami, FL

Miami is often described as a global cruise capital, home to PortMiami — one of the world's busiest cruise ports — and to the corporate headquarters of several of the world's largest cruise lines. Because of this concentration, Miami plays an outsized role in cruise ship accident litigation, regardless of where a passenger boarded or where the injury actually occurred. This guide explains why Miami matters so much to cruise ship injury claims, and what injured passengers should generally know. It is general legal information, not legal advice about any specific injury.

## Why Miami Is Central to Cruise Ship Litigation

Several of the world's largest cruise lines are headquartered in the Miami area, and their standard passenger ticket contracts commonly designate the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida — based in Miami — as the exclusive venue for lawsuits arising from a passenger's injury, regardless of where the passenger lives or where the cruise departed from. The U.S. Supreme Court upheld the general enforceability of these forum-selection clauses in *Carnival Cruise Lines, Inc. v. Shute*, 499 U.S. 585 (1991). As a result, a large share of all U.S. cruise ship injury litigation is concentrated in Miami's federal court, which has developed substantial experience and a well-established body of case law specific to maritime passenger claims.

## Statute of Limitations

Cruise ticket contracts generally cannot shorten the notice period below six months or the filing deadline below one year, under 46 U.S.C. § 30508, though the specific terms in your ticket contract control and should be reviewed as soon as possible after an injury. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to live in Miami to bring a claim there?** No — many cruise ticket contracts require the lawsuit itself to be filed in Miami's federal court regardless of where the passenger lives, due to the forum-selection clause in the ticket contract.

**Does this apply to every cruise line?** Most major cruise lines headquartered in the Miami area use similar forum-selection provisions, but the exact terms should always be confirmed against your specific ticket contract. See our [cruise line accident claims by brand](/cruise-line-accident-claims-by-brand/) guide.

**What if my cruise departed from a different city?** The departure port generally doesn't change which court your ticket contract designates for a lawsuit — that's determined by the contract terms, not the embarkation port.
