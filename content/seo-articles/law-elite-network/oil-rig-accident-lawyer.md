# Oil Rig Accident Lawyer

Explosions and fires are among the most catastrophic events that can occur on an offshore drilling rig or production platform, often causing serious injuries or fatalities to multiple workers at once. An oil rig accident lawyer helps injured workers and families of those killed in these incidents understand what caused the accident, who may be responsible, and what legal options exist. This guide focuses specifically on rig explosions and fires. For oil rig injuries more broadly, see our [oil rig injury lawyer guide](/oil-rig-injury-lawyer/). This is general legal information, not legal advice about any specific incident.

## Why Rig Explosions and Fires Happen

Offshore drilling and production involve flammable hydrocarbons, high-pressure well systems, and complex mechanical and electrical equipment operating in a confined, remote environment — a combination that makes explosions and fires a recognized catastrophic risk in the industry. Common contributing causes include well control failures (sometimes called blowouts), failure of blowout preventer equipment, gas leaks, equipment malfunction, inadequate maintenance, failure to follow well-control or hot-work safety procedures, and design or manufacturing defects in critical safety equipment.

## A Well-Known Example

The April 2010 Deepwater Horizon explosion in the Gulf of Mexico — which killed 11 workers and led to a prolonged oil spill from the Macondo well — remains one of the most significant offshore disasters in U.S. history and prompted major changes to federal offshore safety regulation, including the creation of the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE) in 2011 to oversee offshore drilling safety separately from resource-leasing functions. While most rig accidents are far smaller in scale, this incident illustrates how failures in well control and safety equipment can escalate quickly into a catastrophic event, and why thorough investigation of safety systems and procedures is central to these cases.

## Investigating a Rig Explosion or Fire

Because these incidents can destroy physical evidence and involve multiple companies working on the same rig, prompt and thorough investigation is critical. An oil rig accident lawyer typically works to obtain well-control and safety equipment records, maintenance and inspection logs, incident and regulatory reports (including any findings from federal agencies such as BSEE or the U.S. Coast Guard), witness statements from surviving crew, and, where necessary, expert analysis of equipment failure or well-control procedures.

## Who May Be Liable

A rig explosion or fire can potentially involve liability by more than one company, including the platform or well operator, the drilling contractor, equipment manufacturers (where a defective component such as a blowout preventer or valve contributed to the incident), and other service contractors on site. Because offshore drilling operations typically involve a layered structure of operators, contractors, and subcontractors, identifying every company whose conduct or equipment may have contributed to the incident is often a central part of the investigation.

## Claims Available to Injured Workers and Families

The specific claims available after a rig explosion or fire depend on the injured or deceased worker's employment status and connection to a vessel. Workers who qualify as Jones Act seamen may have negligence and unseaworthiness claims in addition to maintenance and cure; platform-based workers are often covered under the LHWCA and OCSLA framework, with potential third-party claims against non-employer companies. In the case of a fatality, surviving family members may be able to pursue a wrongful death claim under the applicable framework. See our guides to [Jones Act and seaman's injury rights](/jones-act-seamans-injury-rights/) and [common causes of offshore and oil rig accidents](/causes-of-offshore-oil-rig-accidents/) for more detail.

## Injuries From Rig Explosions and Fires

Injuries from these incidents can include severe burns, smoke inhalation, blast-related trauma, hearing loss, traumatic brain injury, and psychological injury in addition to physical harm. Given the severity of these injuries, thorough and ongoing medical documentation is generally important both for treatment and for supporting a claim.

## What an Oil Rig Accident Lawyer Does

An oil rig accident lawyer typically moves quickly to preserve evidence, coordinates with medical providers treating serious injuries, identifies every company that may share liability, and evaluates the applicable legal framework before pursuing compensation through negotiation or litigation. Because these incidents often draw regulatory investigation as well, a lawyer may also track findings from agencies such as BSEE or the Coast Guard, which can be relevant to establishing what happened.

## Compensation

Compensation after a serious rig explosion or fire may potentially include medical expenses, lost wages and future earning capacity, pain and suffering where available, and, in the case of a fatality, wrongful death damages for surviving family members. No specific outcome or amount can be predicted without a full review of the individual facts and applicable law; see our [offshore injury settlement value guide](/offshore-injury-settlement-value/) for the general factors involved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are rig explosions always caused by negligence?** Not necessarily, but a thorough investigation is generally needed to determine whether equipment failure, inadequate maintenance, or a safety-procedure lapse contributed to the incident.

**Can family members of a worker killed in a rig explosion bring a claim?** Depending on the applicable legal framework, surviving family members may be able to pursue a wrongful death claim; the specific process depends on the worker's employment status and the framework that applies.

**How long does an investigation into a rig explosion typically take?** It varies significantly based on the scale of the incident and whether federal regulators are also investigating; a lawyer can help track the process and preserve evidence in the meantime.
